UniEvo-VL: An On-policy Self-Distillation Training Recipe for Multimodal Model Self-improvement

Paper arXiv cs.CV (recent)

Modern multimodal models bring generation and understanding into a single unified system, which enables them to provide and learn from their own feedback. Motivated by this unified capacity, we introduce UniEvo-VL, a self-evolving framework for multimodal models to learn from this constructive self-correction feedback during test-time compute. Instead of relying on a separately trained, stronger generator as teacher, we use critiques of the generator's own outputs, produced by a fixed vision-language critic, as privileged information. The generator and its EMA copy act as student and teacher under different contexts. The student only sees the vanilla question, while the teacher conditions on the privileged critique. Then training minimizes the per-state divergence between their denoising diffusion distributions over the student's own sampling trajectories. Experiments demonstrate that UniEvo-VL improves the image generation capabilities of multimodal models, while maintaining their sensitivity to additional reflection information. With Qwen-Image-2512, direct-generation performance improves from 0.747 to 0.808 on GenEval using Qwen feedback and from 32.97 to 35.53 on GenEval2 Soft-TIFA using the external Luna critic. Moreover, attempts with more powerful external critics (e.g., GPT5.6-Luna) show that multimodal models with strong judge capabilities can anticipate a higher self-evolving ceiling. Last but not least, mixed text-rendering outcomes show that our self-improvements may not be uniform across different tasks. Our study aims to shed light on the current hot recursive self-improvement research line to enhance the user experience when using multimodal models without paired ground-truth images, human annotation, or task-specific reward functions.

MemLife: Curating and Reasoning over Long-Term Egocentric Video Memories

Paper arXiv cs.CV (recent)

Long-term egocentric video enables personalized AI assistants to reason about daily life. However, as video histories grow to hundreds of hours spanning months or years, reprocessing raw clips for every query becomes computationally prohibitive. Memory systems offer a scalable alternative by compacting videos into text representations, but often fail on practical benchmarks: either the memory does not preserve key evidence, or the retriever fails to locate relevant entries due to retrieval competition in growing search spaces. To address these challenges, we introduce MemLife, a multimodal memory system that constructs entity-grounded, first-person text episodes and retrieves them via a time-indexed agentic reader. Without training or query-time video access, MemLife improves over the strongest training-free baseline by 4.6-12.0% across four long-horizon benchmarks. To further improve memory quality, we propose MemOpt, a reinforcement learning framework that optimizes the memory writer to produce faithful, informative, and retrievable memories. MemOpt consistently improves MemLife by 2.7-5.0% across different video and question distributions, with gains that generalize across writer and reader backbones and memory systems.

Jeveration: System One Models Now Generate

Paper arXiv cs.CV (recent)

System One models such as Jev return decisions through fast, parallel prediction. In this paper, we introduce Jeveration, showing that the same decision paradigm can also generate high-quality images in a single forward pass. Jeveration builds on a frozen vision-language model, repurposing its visual-semantic knowledge for holistic image generation. The key challenge is to maintain global consistency. A single prompt admits many valid images, yet parallel predictions must converge on a coherent whole. We therefore condition all positions on a shared random state to coordinate parallel predictions, so that each draw yields a different but coherent image. Since the model tends to ignore this state under direct image supervision, we first learn a distribution over discrete image codes to guide single-pass generation. Beyond text-to-image generation, we further unify image-to-image generation and dense prediction within the same architecture, so that one checkpoint supports all three tasks, each in one forward pass. Experiments show that Jeveration generates a 512x512 image in 78 ms, up to 122x faster than existing unified models, while maintaining competitive image quality and dense prediction performance.

NoiseRater: Meta-Learned Noise Valuation for Diffusion Model Training

Paper arXiv cs.CV (recent)

Training a diffusion model involves two sources of randomness for each data sample: the timestep and the Gaussian noise realization. The timestep has been studied extensively through scheduling and weighting, whereas the impact of the noise realization at a given timestep is still underexplored. In this work, we examine whether different noise instances are equally informative. We introduce NoiseRater, a network that scores an individual noise instance conditioned on the data sample and timestep. The rater is learned through bilevel optimization, where its scores reweight the diffusion loss in the inner loop, and it is updated to reduce validation loss after the inner-loop updates. Using the trained rater to select training noise, we observe three properties of training noise. First, noise realizations at the same timestep are not equally useful: the rater's top-scored noise improves performance over i.i.d. sampling, while its bottom-scored noise degrades it. Second, this utility is contextual: it depends most strongly on the timestep, and to a lesser extent on the image and the class label. Third, noise selection is complementary to timestep-level design, retaining most of its gain when combined with existing scheduling and weighting schemes. These findings establish instance-level noise valuation as a new axis for understanding and improving diffusion training. Code is available at https://github.com/JoeZhao527/Noise-Rater.

Non-penetrating World Models

Paper arXiv cs.CV (recent)

We study a new problem of preventing object penetration in interactive video world models. To understand how existing models handle this, we introduce NoPeBench, a benchmark that evaluates how world models respond to physical obstacles. Our evaluation reveals that none of the open-source models can reliably prevent such failures. Surprisingly, however, these models already encode the information needed to predict such violations. We find that the hidden states of diffusion transformers (DiTs) can predict whether a candidate action will penetrate an obstacle. Building on this, we introduce NoPeWorld, a lightweight framework that detects and prevents penetration. Specifically, we train a small prediction head on top of a frozen DiT's hidden states to read out if a candidate action is safe or not. Unsafe actions are then replaced with safe ones that best preserve the original intent. Despite its simplicity, NoPeWorld substantially reduces the physical violation rate, averaged over six video world models, from 74% to 16%. Remarkably, our method shows zero-shot generalization to open-domain imagery, third-person viewpoints, and moving obstacles. We publicly release our code, the NoPeBench benchmark, and model weights. Project Page: https://nopeworld.github.io .
